01 / The finding

The famous cases tell you almost nothing. The record elsewhere does.

Every case in this edition was chosen for one reason: people cite it. These are the AI stories told in keynotes, board papers and LinkedIn posts, the ones repeated so often that the detail has worn smooth.

Read the sources rather than the retellings and two things stand out.

The first is where the evidence lives. In almost every case here, the usable proof does not come from the company. It comes from a tribunal ruling, a university audit, a quarterly filing, a Nobel citation, a news investigation or a government refund. The announcements supplied the fame. Somebody else supplied the facts.

The second is harder to argue with. Five levels of handover are rated in this archive, from augmented to replaced. Across the fifteen most famous cases in modern AI, not one reaches replaced. The stories are told as replacement. The record does not contain a completed one. The deepest handover here was reversed in public by the company that made it.

That is not an argument that AI does not work. Two cases in this edition can prove real effects, and one of them won a Nobel Prize. It is an argument that fame and evidence are not the same thing, and that the gap between them is where most of the money gets spent.

Headline factUSD 62.1 million, spent between June 2012 and October 2016.

What was handed over

MD Anderson Cancer Center set out to encode the judgement of its senior oncologists into a decision support tool built on IBM Watson, so that community oncologists elsewhere could treat to the same standard. The original contract was USD 2.4 million for six months. It was extended twelve times.

The lesson

A system that cannot read the records your clinicians actually use is not a decision support tool. It is a demonstration with a budget.

My take

The audit is not about whether Watson was clever. It is about procurement, oversight and a project that never met a patient. That is the part everyone forgets when they cite this one.

The starting point

Cancer care varies enormously by where you happen to live. Encoding specialist judgement and distributing it was a genuinely worthwhile problem, which is why the ambition attracted the coverage it did.

What changed

Nothing clinical. The University of Texas System audit found the tool had not been integrated with MD Anderson's current electronic records system, having been piloted on the previous one. IBM's own contractual language stated the Oncology Expert Advisor was not ready for human investigational or clinical use, and that its use in treating patients was prohibited.

What stayed human

Every treatment decision. No patient was treated using the system.

What the number can and cannot show

USD 62.1 million is a documented spend, split roughly USD 39.2 million to IBM and USD 23 million to PricewaterhouseCoopers. It is a cost, not an outcome. The audit examined how the money was spent and took no position on whether the underlying technology worked.

How to measure a similar handover

Before any spend, agree the clinical records integration as a gate rather than a phase. Measure concordance against a specialist panel on live records, and publish the rate at which clinicians accept or override the recommendation.

Sources

University of Texas System Administration, Special Review of Procurement Procedures Related to the MD Anderson Cancer Center Oncology Expert Advisor Project, February 2017. Contemporary reporting in the Houston Chronicle.

The audit document itself is the image. Public record, University of Texas System. Do not use stock photography of hospitals or scientists.

Headline fact4 games to 1, Seoul, March 2016.

What was handed over

Move selection, entirely. For the duration of each game, no human chose a move on the machine's behalf. A DeepMind engineer placed the stones.

The lesson

A narrow task, completely handed over, under rules that never change, is where machines are strongest. Almost no business problem looks like this.

My take

Everyone quotes move 37 as proof that machines are creative. The more useful detail is game four, when Lee Sedol found move 78 and the machine fell apart. Both halves belong in the story.

The starting point

Go had been the standing example of a game too intuitive to brute force. Chess had fallen in 1997. Go was expected to hold for another decade.

What changed

The assumption that intuition was a human reserve. The match was watched by a reported audience in the hundreds of millions across Asia, and it changed what boards were willing to believe was possible.

What stayed human

The framing. Somebody chose Go, chose the opponent, chose the venue and chose to make it public. The demonstration was a communications decision as much as a technical one.

What the number can and cannot show

The match record is complete and uncontested. It proves what happened across five games of Go in March 2016. It says nothing about any commercial task, and the archive carries no business outcome attached to it.

How to measure a similar handover

Treat a demonstration as a demonstration. Before drawing an operational conclusion, ask whether your task has fixed rules, complete information, an unambiguous win condition and unlimited practice. If any answer is no, the demonstration does not transfer.

Sources

Google DeepMind match records and the AlphaGo documentary. Contemporary coverage of the Seoul match, March 2016.

Media note for builder

Official DeepMind match imagery or a still from the documentary, credited to Google DeepMind. Verify licence before use. If no free image is available, report NOT FOUND.

Headline factScrapped. Reported by Reuters in October 2018.

What was handed over

Candidate ranking. The system scored applicants so that recruiters could work from a shortlist.

The lesson

A model trained on a decade of your own hiring will reproduce a decade of your own hiring, including the parts you would not defend out loud.

My take

This case gets cited as a story about bias in AI. It is more precisely a story about bias in the training data, which is to say a story about the company. The machine was accurate about the past. That was the problem.

The starting point

High volume technical recruiting is expensive and slow, and screening is the most repetitive part of it. The case for automating it was obvious.

What changed

According to Reuters, the system penalised CVs containing certain terms associated with women, and Amazon's recruiters were told not to rely on the rankings. The project was abandoned.

What stayed human

The hiring decisions. The tool produced rankings, not offers.

What the number can and cannot show

There is no number here, and that matters. The account rests on a Reuters investigation drawing on people familiar with the project. Amazon has not published a technical account of the system, its performance or its scope. The archive rates the record, not the reporting.

How to measure a similar handover

Audit the training set before the model. Measure outcome rates by group at every stage from screen to offer, run the model shadow against human decisions before it touches a candidate, and publish the override rate.

Sources

Jeffrey Dastin, Reuters, October 2018.

Media note for builder

No authentic image exists. Do not generate one, do not use a lookalike, do not use stock photography of an office. Report NOT FOUND and leave the slot empty. The absence is on theme.

Headline factA USD 304.4 million inventory write-down in the third quarter of 2021, and approximately a 25 per cent reduction in the company's workforce.

What was handed over

Price setting at scale. Zillow bought homes against its own model's estimate of what they would later sell for.

The lesson

When a model's output is a purchase rather than a recommendation, the error does not stay a prediction. It becomes inventory.

My take

The most interesting line in the filing is the word unintentionally. Zillow bought homes at prices higher than its own later estimate of what it could sell them for, and it did so at volume before anyone caught it.

The starting point

Selling a house is slow, uncertain and unpleasant. An instant cash offer is a genuinely good product if the pricing holds.

What changed

On 2 November 2021 the board determined to wind down Zillow Offers, citing home pricing unpredictability, capacity constraints and other operational challenges. The company ended the quarter holding 9,790 homes with a further 8,172 under contract. The full year inventory write-down was later recorded at USD 407.9 million.

What stayed human

The decision to stop. It took a board, not a model, to recognise that the losses were structural.

What the number can and cannot show

These figures are from Zillow's own SEC filings, which is why this case rates higher than most in the edition. They show the financial consequence precisely. They do not isolate how much of the loss belonged to the model, how much to an extraordinary housing market, and how much to capacity constraints in renovation and resale. Zillow's own statement names all three.

How to measure a similar handover

Run the model against a held-out set of transactions before it buys anything. Cap exposure per period. Track realised margin against predicted margin weekly, and set a predefined loss threshold that halts purchasing automatically.

Sources

Zillow Group Form 8-K and Form 10-Q, filed November 2021. Zillow Group Form 10-K for the year ended 31 December 2021.

Media note for builder

The filing is the image. Public record, SEC EDGAR. Alternatively Zillow's own press imagery with licence confirmed.

Headline factFirst place, digital arts category, Colorado State Fair, August 2022.

What was handed over

Image generation. The entrant used Midjourney, then selected, refined and printed the result, and entered it under a category for art created with digital technology.

The lesson

The argument that followed was never really about the prize. It was about who gets called the author, and nobody had agreed an answer before the entry form was filled in.

My take

A county fair set the terms of a global argument about authorship, and it did it before any court or regulator had a view. That is usually how these things start.

The starting point

Generative image tools had become usable by non-specialists within months. The institutions that award, license and credit creative work had rules written for a different technology.

What changed

The question stopped being theoretical. Within a year, disclosure of AI involvement had become a live issue for competitions, publishers, stock libraries and copyright offices.

What stayed human

Selection, iteration, finishing and the decision to enter. The entrant has consistently described a process of many iterations rather than a single prompt.

What the number can and cannot show

A prize is a judgement by a panel under a particular set of rules on a particular day. It is not evidence about the quality of generated images in general, and it tells you nothing about commercial effect.

How to measure a similar handover

If you commission creative work, write the disclosure rule before the brief goes out, not after the work comes back. Define what must be declared, by whom, and what happens if it is not.

Sources

Colorado State Fair Fine Arts competition records, 2022. Contemporary reporting.

Media note for builder

The artwork is the entrant's copyright and is itself the subject of a contested authorship claim. Do not reproduce it. Use the competition's own published results listing as the image, or report NOT FOUND.

Headline factA reported 100 million monthly users within two months of launch, on 30 November 2022.

What was handed over

Nothing, at first. A research preview with a text box. Every subsequent case in this archive is dated from it.

The lesson

Adoption is not deployment. The speed at which people tried it says nothing about what any organisation later managed to hand over successfully.

My take

This is the most cited number in the field and one of the least useful. It counts curiosity. Every genuine question about value sits in the fourteen cases around it.

The starting point

Large language models had existed for years and were largely confined to developers and researchers. The interface, not the model, was the change.

What changed

The conversation moved from research to boardroom inside a quarter. Budgets, job descriptions and product roadmaps followed.

What stayed human

Everything. At launch there was no action it could take, no system it was connected to and no decision it could make on anyone's behalf.

What the number can and cannot show

The 100 million figure is a widely circulated third-party estimate rather than a company disclosure, and user counts of this kind measure reach and nothing else. It is not revenue, retention, task completion or value. It belongs on the bottom rung of the ladder and the builder should present it as such.

How to measure a similar handover

For any new tool, stop counting logins. Measure task completion, time saved against a baseline, error and rework rates, and the proportion of output that survives review unchanged.

Sources

OpenAI launch announcement, 30 November 2022. Widely reported third-party user estimates, February 2023.

Media note for builder

OpenAI's own launch page or product interface screenshot. Credit and date it. Verify licence.

Headline factAI terms written into the Writers Guild and SAG-AFTRA agreements, 2023.

What was handed over

By negotiation, very little. The agreements set out where generative AI may be used in writing and performance, what must be disclosed, and what consent and compensation apply to digital replicas.

The lesson

This is the only case in the edition where a limit was agreed in advance rather than discovered afterwards. Every other case here found its boundary by crossing it.

My take

Two unions did in one year what regulators have not managed since. Whatever you think of the outcome, the mechanism is the interesting part: the people doing the work set the terms before the technology arrived at scale.

The starting point

Generative tools reached usable quality in writing and image generation at the same moment both contracts came up for renewal. The timing was not planned by anyone.

What changed

AI moved from a technical question to a contractual one. The agreements became the reference point for other industries thinking about disclosure and consent.

What stayed human

Authorship and likeness, by written agreement, with specified conditions.

What the number can and cannot show

The agreements are public and their terms are exact. What they cannot show is effect. There is no published measure of how production economics, employment or output have changed as a result, and the archive should not imply one.

How to measure a similar handover

Write the limit before the capability arrives. Specify what must be disclosed, who consents, what is compensated and what is prohibited outright, and review it on a fixed schedule rather than after an incident.

Sources

Writers Guild of America Minimum Basic Agreement, 2023. SAG-AFTRA Television/Theatrical Agreement, 2023.

Media note for builder

The agreements themselves are the image. Picket line photography is overwhelmingly wire agency material and is not free to use. Do not use it unless a licence is confirmed in writing.

Headline factThe author profiles were removed from the site after Futurism approached the publisher, in November 2023.

What was handed over

Product review content, supplied by a third party, published under bylines belonging to people who could not be found, with portraits traced to a marketplace selling AI-generated headshots.

The lesson

The reputational damage attached to the fake person, not to the use of a tool. Nobody minded the software. They minded being told a human wrote it.

My take

Be precise about what was proved here. The invented authors and the generated headshots are documented. Whether the articles themselves were machine-written was denied by the publisher and never established.

The starting point

A magazine with one of the strongest mastheads in American publishing, under an owner pursuing commerce revenue at volume.

What changed

The Arena Group said the content came from a third party, AdVon Commerce, which had assured it the articles were written by humans using pen names, and ended that partnership. The chief executive was later terminated. The company subsequently repositioned itself as an AI business.

What stayed human

The reporting. A journalist asking who the authors were is what surfaced the whole thing.

What the number can and cannot show

There is no outcome figure. What exists is a documented investigation, a company statement disputing part of it, and a sequence of departures. The archive rates that record as archival, and the disputed element is marked rather than resolved.

How to measure a similar handover

Make authorship a contractual warranty with your suppliers, not an assumption. Audit bylines against real people. Publish a disclosure standard and apply it to licensed content as well as your own.

Sources

Maggie Harrison Dupre, Futurism, 27 November 2023. The Arena Group public statement, 28 November 2023. Contemporary reporting on subsequent executive changes.

Media note for builder

Do not reproduce the AI-generated headshots. Do not reproduce Sports Illustrated covers or mastheads. Use The Arena Group's own published statement as the image, or report NOT FOUND.

Headline factCAD 812.02, ordered 14 February 2024.

What was handed over

Answering customer questions about fare policy. The airline's website chatbot told a passenger he could buy a full fare ticket and apply for the bereavement rate retroactively within ninety days. The airline's actual policy, on a page the chatbot linked to, said the opposite.

The lesson

If it speaks on your website, it speaks for you. The tribunal rejected the suggestion that the chatbot was a separate legal entity responsible for its own actions.

My take

The sum is trivial and the reasoning is not. Air Canada argued its own chatbot was somebody else. The tribunal member called that a remarkable submission, and the question of who owns a machine's promises has been settled that way ever since.

The starting point

A passenger booking travel after a family death, trying to understand a policy that exists precisely for that situation.

What changed

The tribunal found negligent misrepresentation, holding that Air Canada did not take reasonable care to ensure its chatbot was accurate. The award was CAD 650.88 in damages, CAD 36.14 in pre-judgment interest and CAD 125 in tribunal fees.

What stayed human

The adjudication. A tribunal member read both pages and decided which one the customer was entitled to rely on.

What the number can and cannot show

The ruling is exact, published and citable, which is why this rates as a documented record rather than a claim. Two limits matter. The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal is a small claims body with a monetary limit, so this is persuasive reasoning rather than binding precedent. And Air Canada's own spokesperson indicated the chatbot was not a generative system, so this is a case about liability for automated statements, not about generative AI specifically.

How to measure a similar handover

Treat every customer-facing statement the system can make as published policy. Test it against the policy pages weekly, log every answer, and give the customer a visible route to a human.

Sources

Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149, British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal, 14 February 2024, member Christopher C. Rivers.

Media note for builder

The decision is the image. Public record, CanLII. Do not use stock photography of aircraft or call centres.

Headline factKlarna reported its AI assistant handled two-thirds of service chats in its first month, equivalent to the work of 700 full-time agents.

What was handed over

Front-line customer service, at volume, with the escalation path to a human narrowed to the point where it stopped functioning as one.

The lesson

The average held while the thing that mattered broke. Routine volume was large enough to hide deterioration on the cases where the customer was in distress.

My take

This is the most cited enterprise AI case study there is, and for once the citation is deserved, because it is one of the very few with a complete public arc: a confident launch, specific numbers, and a reversal the chief executive stated himself.

The starting point

A high-volume consumer finance business with heavy, expensive and largely outsourced customer service.

What changed

By November 2025 Klarna put the figure at 853 agents and USD 60 million saved. The company then moved back towards a hybrid model, rebuilding human capacity and repositioning access to a person as the premium service. A Forrester analyst told CX Dive the company had overpivoted to cost containment and described it as almost the poster child for bad AI deployment.

What stayed human

In the end, the cases that decide whether a customer stays: disputes, complex refunds and financial hardship.

What the number can and cannot show

Every figure here is company-reported and the agent-equivalent number describes workload absorbed, not 700 people made redundant. The reduction in overall headcount came through attrition and a hiring freeze across the business, not a customer service redundancy programme. No independent audit of the savings, the satisfaction decline or the cost of rebuilding has been published.

How to measure a similar handover

Stop reporting average satisfaction. Segment by case type and track the hard cases separately. Measure the escalation rate, the time to reach a human, and resolution on disputes and hardship cases. Model the cost of reversing the decision before you make it.

Live note

Review at least twice a year. The relevant questions are whether the hybrid model has stabilised, whether any independent figures have been published, and whether the savings claim has been restated.

Sources

Klarna company statements, 2024 and 2025. CX Dive reporting, including the November 2025 figures and the Forrester comment. Contemporary reporting on the reversal.

Media note for builder

Klarna's own press imagery or a credited still from a recorded interview with the chief executive. Verify licence.

Headline factPredicted structures for virtually all of the roughly 200 million proteins researchers have identified. The Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024 was awarded one half to David Baker for computational protein design, and the other half jointly to Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for protein structure prediction.

What was handed over

Structure prediction from amino acid sequence, end to end. A problem that had stood for about fifty years.

The lesson

This is what a handover looks like when the output can be checked against reality. The predictions were measurable against experimentally determined structures, so the claim could be tested rather than asserted.

My take

The only case in this edition that can prove itself, and note what made that possible: an open database, a public benchmark, and a field that had spent decades building the ground truth to test against. The proof was not a marketing decision.

The starting point

Determining a protein structure experimentally took enormous time and cost. Over about sixty years, biologists had determined the structures of roughly 190,000 proteins.

What changed

EMBL partnered with Google DeepMind to make the predictions freely available through the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database. The database launched in 2021 with just over 360,000 structures and grew to around 200 million, covering over one million organisms. The Nobel citation records use by more than two million people across 190 countries.

What stayed human

Experimental validation, and the decades of curated open data the system was trained on. The Nobel laureate who led the work has said publicly that public data were essential to its development.

What the number can and cannot show

A prediction is a prediction. The database publishes a per-residue confidence score for exactly this reason, and a low-confidence region is not a solved structure. The count of 200 million describes coverage, not accuracy, and accuracy varies. What the archive rates highly is not the size of the number but the existence of an independent benchmark against which the claim could fail.

How to measure a similar handover

Find or build the ground truth before you build the system. Publish the confidence score alongside every output, and make the failure modes as visible as the successes.

Sources

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024 press release, 9 October 2024. EMBL-EBI and Google DeepMind, AlphaFold Protein Structure Database.

Media note for builder

EMBL-EBI publishes AlphaFold structure imagery with clear credit lines, for example Karen Arnott, EMBL-EBI. Confirm the licence and reproduce the credit exactly.

Headline factIn System1 audience testing, the 2025 advert scored a maximum 5.9-star rating and 97 per cent on distinctiveness.

What was handed over

Production of the brand's most recognisable annual advertising. The 2024 campaign was made with three AI studios. The 2025 campaign returned to the approach with animals rather than people, which sidestepped the uncanny valley problem of the first attempt.

The lesson

The audience that shouts and the audience that is measured are not the same audience, and in this case they disagreed completely.

My take

This is the most instructive case in the edition, because it contains two credible numbers pointing in opposite directions. A social media backlash in the millions of impressions, and a formal audience test scoring the work at the top of the scale. Pick whichever one suits your argument and you have learned nothing.

The starting point

An advert first broadcast in 1995 with enormous accumulated affection, and a production process the chief marketing officer described as previously starting a year in advance.

What changed

The timeline collapsed to about a month. A small team generated and refined tens of thousands of video clips in thirty days. The company publicly described the 2024 work as a milestone and returned to the method for a second year despite the reaction.

What stayed human

Direction and selection. The company's generative AI lead has described human storytellers overseeing the work to keep it emotionally coherent, and the wider campaign still included conventionally produced films with human actors.

What the number can and cannot show

The System1 result is a structured audience test with a published methodology, which places it above a view count on the ladder. It measures predicted brand effect from a sample response. It does not measure sales, and it does not capture the reputational cost among the creative industry, which is where most of the criticism came from. Social backlash volume is not on the ladder at all.

How to measure a similar handover

Run the formal test and the sentiment tracking side by side, and report both. Separate craft criticism from brand effect, because they answer different questions. Then measure sales.

Sources

Coca-Cola press materials, 2024 and 2025. System1 audience testing as reported in The Grocer. Wall Street Journal interview with the chief marketing officer. Contemporary reporting on the reaction.

Media note for builder

A still from the official Coca-Cola advert on the company's own channel, credited to Coca-Cola, with the licence basis stated. The screening room should link to the original upload.

Headline factThe AI Pin business was shut down in February 2025, with HP acquiring most of the company's assets for USD 116 million, after Humane had raised a reported USD 230 million.

What was handed over

The interface itself. The proposition was that a screenless assistant could take over the everyday tasks people use a phone for.

The lesson

A demonstration optimised for a stage is not a product. The gap only appears when a reviewer uses it as their actual phone for a week.

My take

What killed it was not the concept. It was the distance between the launch film and the thing in your hand, and the fact that a single reviewer was willing to say so plainly.

The starting point

Significant funding, senior founders from Apple and a widely watched launch presentation.

What changed

Independent reviews, most prominently Marques Brownlee's, were severely negative on latency, reliability and heat. The business closed within about a year of shipping.

What stayed human

Judgement, exercised in public by reviewers rather than by the company or its investors.

What the number can and cannot show

The asset sale figure and the capital raised are both reported and give a defensible picture of value destroyed. They do not isolate the cause. Hardware businesses fail for supply chain, pricing, distribution and timing reasons as well as product ones, and no post mortem has been published separating them.

How to measure a similar handover

Put the product in the hands of people who will use it instead of their existing tool, not alongside it, before launch. Measure task completion and abandonment in daily use, and treat latency as a product requirement rather than an engineering detail.

Related case

Marques Brownlee's review appears in Creator Turning Points, Edition 02, as a case about a reviewer's influence. This case is about the handover. Cross-link both ways.

Sources

Humane and HP announcements, February 2025. Contemporary reporting on the acquisition and the capital raised.

Media note for builder

Official Humane or HP product imagery with licence confirmed. Do not use a reviewer's video thumbnail.

Headline factA partial refund of the AUD 440,000 paid by the Australian government, agreed in October 2025.

What was handed over

Drafting support on a 237-page independent assurance review for the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations, examining the IT system used to automate penalties in Australia's welfare system.

The lesson

The output was fluent, structured and properly formatted, which is exactly why nobody checked it. Fluency is not accuracy, and a citation is a claim like any other.

My take

Note the subject of the report. A review of an automated penalty system, undermined by unverified automated output. The irony is not the point, but it is hard to ignore.

The starting point

A Big Four firm delivering an assurance review to a government department on a system with a serious history behind it.

What changed

A Sydney University researcher, Chris Rudge, found the report full of fabricated references and alerted the media, reporting up to twenty errors in the original version. These included a fabricated quote from a federal court judgment and citations to academic papers that do not exist. A revised version was published in October 2025, and it included a disclosure that a generative AI language system, Azure OpenAI, had been used in writing the report. The department said Deloitte had confirmed some footnotes and references were incorrect and had agreed to repay the final instalment under its contract.

What stayed human

One researcher, reading closely. That is the entire control that worked.

What the number can and cannot show

The refund is a documented commercial consequence, although the exact amount repaid was to be made public only after reimbursement. One important limit: Deloitte did not confirm that the errors were AI-generated, and the AI disclosure appeared in the revised version. The causal link is strongly implied by the sequence and is not formally established, and the archive should mark it that way.

How to measure a similar handover

Make citation verification a separate, named step with its own sign-off, performed by someone who did not draft the document. Count errors found per hundred references and report it to the client.

Sources

Australian Department of Employment and Workplace Relations statement, October 2025. Associated Press reporting, 7 October 2025. Australian Financial Review reporting, August 2025. The revised report as published on the department's website.

Media note for builder

The published report on the department's website is the image. Public record. Do not use Deloitte branding or office photography.

Headline factVoice AI was live in more than 500 US drive-thrus and had taken over two million orders at the point the company said it was rethinking the rollout.

What was handed over

Order taking at the drive-thru, without a human intermediary, on a platform with menu logic attached.

The lesson

A system open to the public will be probed by the public. The viral failures were not edge cases discovered by accident. They were found deliberately, by customers trying to reach a human.

My take

This case has already aged against the story everyone tells about it. The meme is that Taco Bell pulled back. The record says it expanded, and that is exactly why cases like this need a review date on them.

The starting point

Drive-thru ordering is high-volume, repetitive and noisy, and it is a bottleneck at peak. The case for voice automation was sound.

What changed

In August 2025, after a run of viral videos including an order for 18,000 cups of water, the chief digital and technology officer told the Wall Street Journal the company was reconsidering where to use the system, and said staff would be coached on when to step in. The deployment then grew rather than shrank. By late 2026 the voice AI was running at roughly 900 restaurants with a stated target of 1,500 by year end. On a November 2026 earnings call, executives said drive-thru satisfaction at those locations had improved by 6 per cent and sales had risen 1.5 per cent through automatic upsells.

What stayed human

Escalation, by design after the fact. Staff monitor the system and take over at peak and when it struggles.

What the number can and cannot show

The improvement figures are company-reported on an earnings call, with no published methodology and no independent verification. Asked directly about the effect on franchisee profits, the company did not answer directly. A separate claim about improved employee retention has been made by company and vendor statements without published figures. The viral failures, by contrast, are visible and verifiable, which is a useful reminder that the most watchable evidence and the most reliable evidence are rarely the same thing.

How to measure a similar handover

Red-team the system against deliberate abuse before launch, with sanity limits on quantity and value. Measure order accuracy, repeat interactions and abandonment at the lane rather than satisfaction in aggregate. Publish the method alongside the number.

Live note

Review quarterly. The open questions are whether the satisfaction and sales figures are ever substantiated independently, whether the 1,500 target is met, and whether franchisee economics are disclosed.

Sources

Wall Street Journal interview with Taco Bell's chief digital and technology officer, August 2025. Restaurant Dive and Restaurant Business reporting, 2026. Yum! Brands earnings call, November 2026.

Media note for builder

05 / The evidence

What counts as proof

Each case carries one evidence rating. The rating describes the best public source, not the quality of the work.

Modelled effectAn effect estimated against a baseline or an independent benchmark that the claim could have failed.
Sales trendA financial or commercial figure moved, with other causes not separated out.
SurveyA structured sample reported a change in what they believe, intend or notice.
Reach onlyThe number counts attention, not outcomes.
ArchivalThe record shows what happened, without an outcome figure.

Edition 01 distribution. Modelled effect 1. Sales trend 4. Survey 1. Reach only 1. Archival 8.

The handover scale

A second rating, carried by every case, describing how deep the handover went.

AugmentedThe machine drafts. A human decides every output.
AssistedThe machine does one step. Humans run the process.
DelegatedThe machine runs the task. Humans sample and correct.
AutomatedThe machine runs the task. Humans handle exceptions only.
ReplacedThe human function no longer exists.

Edition 01 distribution. Augmented 4. Assisted 3. Delegated 3. Automated 5. Replaced 0.

How cases earn a place

Each case has a specific thing handed to a machine, a traceable public record, and a consequence somebody documented. The collection is curated and unranked. The measurement suggestions are proposals for a comparable decision today, not findings.

What this edition rates, and what it does not

The archive rates the public record. It does not rate the technology, it does not rate the vendors, and it does not rate anyone's integrity. A deployment that went wrong is evidence about that deployment, not evidence that AI does not work.

06 / The editorial purpose

Say what was handed over.

Most writing about AI describes a capability. This archive describes a decision: somebody chose to hand a specific piece of work to a machine, somebody else lived with the result, and a record exists of what happened next. The cases are dated and the live ones carry a review date, because this is a story still being written and a judgement made in 2026 should be allowed to age in public.
